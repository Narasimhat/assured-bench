# Functional knockout selection v2

The existing design engine generates the plans. The new selector ranks single guides first and then evaluates a coordinated pair. This is a sequence-supported recommendation, not a newly trained model or a calibrated probability of functional knockout.

## Eligibility and individual ranking

Scan the supplied reference for the selected enzyme. Exclude spacers with GC below 30% or above 80%, calculated from the exact spacer fraction (excluding PAM and scaffold/repeat); inside that range GC is a penalty, not a filter (see below). Require no homopolymer longer than four bases, and no TTTT when delivery is U6/Pol III. A cut must be more than 10 bp inside a coding segment in every usable same-gene CDS annotation supplied with the record. The conservative boundary margin keeps the existing deletion panel, which includes 10 bp deletions, inside annotated coding sequence. Criteria are not relaxed when too few guides qualify.

**Version 3.0.0, one selector.** Version 2 excluded every spacer outside 40–60% GC. The literature does not support a window that narrow: very low (≤35%) and very high (>80%) overall GC are the settings in which guides are less active (Wang 2014 and Doench 2014 as summarised in the sgRNA-motif study, Cell Reports 2019; in sgDesigner, Bioinformatics 2020, most guides above 80% or below 30% were non-functional), while one independent dataset found only a minor, non-significant gain for 41–70% over below 40% or above 70% (Labuhn 2018, Nucleic Acids Research). Version 3 therefore keeps the extremes out (below 30%, above 80%) and subtracts a penalty from the predicted loss of function used for ranking: 0 points at 40–60%, 5 points at 35–40% and 60–70%, 10 points at 30–35% and 70–80%. The unit is 5 points because that is the finest difference the repair-outcome predictor resolves (Lindel's own test-set correlation is 0.71). The penalty sizes are not calibrated. Every selection records whether its pair survives doubling them (`koSelection.gcSensitivity`) and, if not, which pair a doubled penalty would choose; the report prints that as the one alternative. Replace the penalty with an on-target model once lab outcomes can compare them.

The Co-Scientist panel no longer chooses guides by a second rule. It passes the pan-isoform exons to this selector (`commonKoExons`: within a functional tier, guides in those exons rank first), and its ranking table is ordered by the same single-guide ranking (`rankKoSingles`); guides the selector excludes are listed after the ranked ones without a rank.

Rank by coding coverage, existing frameshift-panel functional tier, pan-isoform exon when supplied, escape in other annotated coding sequences, and supported repair predictions after the GC penalty in five-percentage-point bins. Potential in-frame exon skipping, a sequence self-complementarity screen, distance from coding boundaries, and balanced GC break remaining ties. Exon length divisible by three is a rescue flag, not evidence that skipping occurs. Individual tiers remain conditional on frameshift outcomes and are not cutting efficiencies.

The functional panel already checks NMD escape, retained truncated protein and possible downstream translation initiation. SpCas9 can additionally use loaded Lindel weights. Cas9 repair predictions are never transferred to other enzymes. Distinct same-gene CDS structures are deduplicated; the previous eight-structure cap has been removed. Unsupported or absent transcript annotations cannot establish full biological isoform coverage.

## Individual guides versus the pair

The result records the top two individuals, their combined nominal deletion assessment and up to six backup individual guides. Pair search retains the top two, adds high-ranked guides with cut-position diversity, and examines all combinations in a maximum 24-guide shortlist. Cuts must be 10–140 bp apart; 40–140 bp is preferred among equivalent functional assessments. Pair tier, pan-isoform exon and isoform escape precede the pair's predicted loss of function (weaker guide first, then both), which precedes spacing preference and individual ranks. The coordinated pair can differ from the top individuals. The design's guides, primers, ordering and QC export describe this coordinated pair; the report identifies the difference explicitly.

If no coordinated pair qualifies, return a diagnostic with the individual rankings in `koSelection`; do not silently widen spacing or GC. Pair search is bounded and disclosed, not an assertion of global optimality. Cas12 cut positions are nominal; staggered-end processing can change the repaired junction.

## Reports and traceability

The HTML and text reports include eligibility counts, ranking policy/version, individual recommendations, pair assessment, backups, evidence scope, missing assessments and validation advice. The QC handoff records selection version and policy without changing its coordinate system. Manual guide overrides must meet the sequence and shared coding-interior requirements and have 30–80% spacer GC; they are assessed but are not falsely labelled automatically optimized.

Expression in the user's cells, essential-domain annotations, sample variants/copy number, genome-wide specificity, calibrated cutting efficiency, folding energies and rearrangement/regulatory effects are explicitly unassessed. Existing primers support the coordinated design; sequencing both sites/junction and confirming protein loss plus relevant function remain necessary. Published-dataset benchmarking and experimental validation have not been performed for this selector.

## Choosing a pair by hand

A knockout design lists the guides the selector ranked (`result.koPicker`: up to 60 rows, in the selector's order, each with its tier, GC and penalty, cut position, and the repair outcomes Lindel predicts: loss of function, frameshift, +1 insertion, in-frame, and the share that leaves other isoforms intact). The Design screen shows them under "Choose the guide pair" and can sort them by predicted loss of function after the GC penalty.

Ticking two guides previews the pair before anything is regenerated (`previewKoPair`): the nominal deletion between the cuts and whether it shifts the frame, the pair's functional tier, the weaker guide's predicted loss of function and the fraction of cells with both alleles lost if every cell is edited, and the warnings the report will carry (cuts outside the 10–140 bp window the selector searches, guides in different exons, a deletion over 1 kb, a GC penalty, a weaker guide under 60%). "Use this pair" regenerates the design, primers, report and exports for it; the isoform, protein and ClinVar lookups of the earlier run are reused, so only the pair-dependent steps run again.

The report says the pair was chosen by the user, names the selector's recommendation and gives the weaker guide's predicted loss of function for both (`koPairChoice`). A pair that happens to equal the selector's is not reported as a user choice. "Back to the selector's pair" runs the selection again.
